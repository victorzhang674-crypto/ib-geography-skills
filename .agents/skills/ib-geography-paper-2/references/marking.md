# Paper 2 marking reference

Source: IB *Geography guide*, first assessment 2019, updated November 2022, pp. 56, 58–62 and 82–84. See the [IB specimen papers and markschemes](https://www.ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/assessment-and-exams/sample-exam-papers/) for question-level examples.

## Sections A and B

Use the analytic allocation for each subpart. Identify each distinct point or valid alternative. Check whether credit requires development, a location or other specific evidence. Apply the item's own rules for units, rounding, tolerances and marking linked parts. In B, ground the decision in the visual itself. A correct general fact about climate, population or resources cannot establish that the student read a particular feature from the figure.

Separate observation from inference. `Identify` asks for the feature or value, `describe` for a detailed account of the pattern, and `explain` for the reasons or causes. Check whether the prompt asks for a difference in levels, a change over time or a difference in rates.

## Section C, /10

Apply one integer best-fit mark across knowledge and understanding, application and analysis, synthesis and evaluation, and organization and geographical skills. Use the question-specific indicative content to determine relevance and accept valid alternative evidence. Compare adjacent bands if close. Choose the upper mark when the response demonstrates the band qualities more consistently.

| Band | Practical reading of the guide |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No evidence meets the lowest descriptor. |
| 1–2 | Fragmentary or mostly irrelevant material, with little accurate geographical knowledge and no meaningful analysis or structure. |
| 3–4 | General material with a mix of relevant and irrelevant facts. Links are asserted, analysis misses the question, and organization is weak. |
| 5–6 | Addresses part of the question with some relevant evidence. Analysis is narrow, alternative views or evidence limitations are mostly listed, and the judgment has limited support. |
| 7–8 | Covers the main demands with accurate evidence, clear analysis and logical organization. Describes alternative perspectives or evidence limitations, though the overall argument or conclusion remains uneven. |
| 9–10 | Answers the precise question in depth with integrated evidence, explained mechanisms, balanced evaluation and a justified conclusion where appropriate. Handles complexity, exceptions or comparisons in a way that advances the argument. |

Check whether comparisons use like-for-like measures and whether each case illustrates the geographical mechanism claimed. Check any diagrams for conventions and contribution to the argument when appropriate. Link feedback to what the prompt demands. A comparison between two core topics earns its place when it helps explain or weigh the issue asked about.

For `discuss`, consider a range of arguments and support the conclusion. For `evaluate`, weigh strengths and limitations. For `to what extent`, make the degree of agreement and its conditions clear.
