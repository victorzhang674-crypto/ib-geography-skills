# Paper 3 marking reference

Source: IB *Geography guide*, first assessment 2019, updated November 2022, pp. 56, 59, 62–68 and 82–84. See the [IB specimen papers and markschemes](https://www.ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/assessment-and-exams/sample-exam-papers/) for question-specific guidance in context.

Award one best-fit integer for each part. Read the matching indicative content to interpret the specific demand and consider valid alternatives. Compare adjacent bands where evidence is mixed, then place the answer within the chosen band by how well and consistently it meets the descriptor. The assessment objectives are dimensions of each band. For example, a well-written response with extensive case detail may still sit below the top band if it misses a key clause of the prompt.

## Part (a), /12

| Band | Practical reading of the guide |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No evidence meets the lowest descriptor. |
| 1–3 | General or disconnected material with little question-specific evidence, analysis or organization. |
| 4–6 | Addresses part of the question or misses the command term. Evidence is mixed in relevance, links are asserted and organization is weak. |
| 7–9 | Addresses most demands with mostly accurate, relevant evidence. Analysis is outlined and may be largely descriptive. Paragraphs can read as separate points. |
| 10–12 | Addresses all demands and the command term. Integrates detailed evidence into a well-linked explanation, uses precise geographical terminology and draws the answer together. |

Formal synthesis or evaluation is unnecessary at every band in (a). Where the question calls for more than one side or category, look for clear explanation of the relationships and evidence for each.

## Part (b), /16

| Band | Practical reading of the guide |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No evidence meets the lowest descriptor. |
| 1–4 | General or disconnected material, with little applied evidence or organization and no defensible perspective or meaningful syllabus links. |
| 5–8 | Partial coverage with mixed evidence, weak structure, limited perspective and links concentrated on one topic. Synthesis or evaluation is unnecessary at this band. |
| 9–12 | Most demands are met with relevant evidence and described links to multiple topics. There is synthesis or evaluation. Alternatives may be listed and the perspective may be supported only in general terms. |
| 13–16 | All demands are met with a coherent, evidence-led analysis. The answer explains relevant links across topics or subtopics, synthesizes and evaluates evidence, considers alternative interpretations and justifies its judgment with attention to evidence certainty. |

For feedback, distinguish extra case detail from a genuine improvement in reasoning. For (a), point to the causal link or part of the prompt that needs explanation. For (b), point to the evidence or perspective that could change the judgment and show where the student should weigh it.
