# From local fieldwork question to IA report

Based on the IB *Geography guide*, first assessment 2019, updated November 2022, pp. 69–80. The section budgets below are suggestions from the guide. They total about 2,450 words within the 2,500-word cap.

## Design before collecting

- Start with a relationship, spatial pattern, or comparison that can be measured at specific accessible sites. Name the relevant syllabus topic and explain why geographic theory predicts the expected pattern. Use a hypothesis if it helps test the question. A local study can examine a wider issue through field evidence collected at its sites.
- For every variable, record its definition, units, instrument or observation protocol, sites, timing, sample selection and planned repetitions. Decide which nuisance variables should be held comparable or recorded. Pilot the method to check that its results will be usable and that the analysis fits the sampling design. Follow school fieldwork procedures and account for access, safety, consent and privacy as relevant.
- Example planning prompt for Option G (Urban environments). “How does the daytime surface-temperature difference between shaded and sun-exposed pavement vary across three public squares in [local area]?” Plan paired readings on comparable surfaces within the same time windows. Record weather, paving material and site differences. Use the observations to test the strength and consistency of the shade effect and to assess where the design limits the inference.

## Build the report

| Criterion and suggested words | What the student should develop |
| --- | --- |
| A · 300 | State the focused fieldwork question, site and syllabus link. Explain a theory-based expectation where useful. Locate the study area and sample sites on a relevant map with title, scale, orientation, legend and source as applicable. |
| B · 300 | Explain what was measured, where, when and how, including sampling, instruments and repetitions where relevant. Justify why the design produces evidence suited to the question and identify changes from the planned protocol. |
| C · 500 | Check units, missing values, coding and transformations. Match the display to the question and data. A site map can show spatial pattern, a paired dot plot can show site comparisons, and a scatterplot can show the relationship between quantitative variables. Number, title, label and reference figures. Integrate displays with the analysis in the main report. |
| D · 850 | For each main finding, point to the figure, quantify or substantiate the pattern, explain a geographical mechanism and relate it to the question. Compare sites or groups, address anomalies where present, and explain how location, method or uncertainty affects interpretation. Choose statistical tools only when the design, data and assumptions support them. |
| E · 200 | Answer the fieldwork question directly, indicating direction, extent and any important exception. Use specific results already analyzed and revisit the hypothesis where relevant. |
| F · 300 | Evaluate the question, location, collection and presentation. For each important issue, explain how it affected validity, reliability or interpretation. Propose a practical improvement and explain its expected effect. Distinguish an improvement to the same design from an extension to a new site or question. |

A writing scaffold for D is **pattern → quantified or specific evidence → mechanism → alternative explanation or exception → implication for the fieldwork question**. In the pavement example, the student could compare paired temperature differences, then check whether a different material or a change in cloud cover coincided with any exception. A pattern in a small local sample supports a conclusion at the sampled sites and times.

## Word count and presentation

Count the main text wherever it appears, including the fieldwork question, analysis, conclusion and evaluation. Count annotations over 10 words and footnotes over 15 words. Exclude the title page, acknowledgments, contents, headings, references, map keys, labels of at most 10 words, brief footnotes of at most 15 words, data or category tables, calculations and raw-data or calculation appendices. Keep appendices limited to relevant raw material, calculations or representative instruments as appropriate. Keep essential figures and analysis in the body and number the report pages. Reference supplementary sources and base the findings on the student's field evidence.
