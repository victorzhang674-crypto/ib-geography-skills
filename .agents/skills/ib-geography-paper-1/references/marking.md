# Paper 1 marking reference

Source: IB *Geography guide*, first assessment 2019, updated November 2022, pp. 56, 58–62 and 82–84. See the [IB specimen papers and markschemes](https://www.ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/assessment-and-exams/sample-exam-papers/) for worked examples of question-level marking.

## Short parts

Use the exact analytic markscheme when supplied. Check what each credit is for and whether development or a second distinct point is required. Apply any caps, units, tolerances, valid alternatives or source restrictions specified for the item. Check evidence directly against the original figure and credit each idea only as allowed by the scheme. For example, a location and direction can make a pattern claim precise, while a mechanism explains how the pattern arose. A generic claim can be plausible yet fail to identify the pattern shown in that resource.

For `state` or `identify`, look for a specific, concise answer. `Describe` calls for features or patterns, while `explain` calls for reasons or causes. For example, in a practice /2 item that credits one cause and one development, “more paved surfaces” supplies a cause and “lower infiltration sends more rainfall into surface runoff” develops it. Apply the real item's allocation when it differs.

## Extended response, /10

Award a single integer using best fit across knowledge and understanding, application and analysis, synthesis and evaluation, and organization and geographical skills. The assessment objectives are dimensions of one band. Use the indicative content to interpret what the question calls for and credit other valid approaches under the scheme. Where the response is between two bands, compare both. Within the chosen band, use the upper mark if the qualities are demonstrated more consistently.

| Band | Practical reading of the guide |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No evidence meets the lowest descriptor. |
| 1–2 | Fragmentary or mostly irrelevant material, with little accurate geographical knowledge and no meaningful analysis or structure. |
| 3–4 | General material with a mix of relevant and irrelevant facts. Links are asserted, analysis misses the question, and organization is weak. |
| 5–6 | Addresses part of the question with some relevant evidence. Analysis is narrow, alternative views or evidence limitations are mostly listed, and the judgment has limited support. |
| 7–8 | Covers the main demands with accurate evidence, clear analysis and logical organization. Describes alternative perspectives or evidence limitations, though the overall argument or conclusion remains uneven. |
| 9–10 | Answers the precise question in depth with integrated evidence, explained mechanisms, balanced evaluation and a justified conclusion where appropriate. Handles complexity, exceptions or comparisons in a way that advances the argument. |

Check diagram quality and interpretation when the question or argument calls for a diagram. To diagnose an essay stuck at 5–6, identify the part of the question still unanswered and show where a case fact could be turned into an explanation or a comparison into a defensible judgment.

For `examine`, probe relationships and assumptions. For `discuss`, develop a balanced review. For `evaluate` or `to what extent`, weigh the evidence and state a defensible degree of agreement or a judgment with relevant conditions.
